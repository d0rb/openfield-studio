# Design System: OpenField Studio

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "The Cinema Lobby"**

OpenField Studio is a cinema lobby at night with the screening room behind its doors. The Lobby, the first of seven rooms, opens on the trailer running the width of the room, a cut of recorded takes with their own sound and title cards in the site's own type. A chip on the picture names the take on screen and opens it, the two actions stand on the picture's foot at the right, and a reel of marquee bulbs under the screen lights along each cut as it plays. On the floor under the screen the headline sits beside a rendered letter board that hangs real letters one by one and swaps its middle line between the counts the room can play: the camera moves, the effects and the recorded takes. Below it the wall hangs every app as a one-sheet in a lit poster case. Square key art rendered on our own GPU falls away into the dark of the sheet, the title sits in tall condensed capitals in the lower third, and a billing block names the models and how long it has been showing. Under the pointer or keyboard focus a poster comes alive into its recorded take with a Recorded take stamp and an orange ticket stub, and a few posters at a time wake on their own. Then come the galleries. Every camera move that held plays its take in a smaller case, the mouse scrubs the take so the hand drives the camera, and each move opens a page of its own. The effects run on a rail, Name the move plays in the room, the programme and the latest takes are lobby cards printed on card stock, the latest takes drift past like film through a gate, and the credits say where every picture came from.

Behind the doors the stations (Cast, Direct, Shots, Re-shoot, Takes) are the screening room, and they stay the quiet virtual-production stage they were. The footage plays on an LED wall with hairline frame guides, a burned-in timecode and a tally lamp. The camera move is the set seen from above and to one side, with the camera travelling its path in step with the take and numbered tape marks lighting as the shot reaches each approved still. The settings read as a camera report. The lobby enters the stations only as atoms: the primary action is a roll ticket with notched ends, the Take panel's receipt is a ticket stub on card stock, render progress is a row of marquee bulbs, the rail's signs are rendered props, and an empty room shows its rendered set before its words. The ground is lacquer black, warm, never blue. The interface owns four colours: lacquer black and its charcoal steps, marquee white on the letter board, ticket orange for the one action under the eye, and brass for trim and passes. Every other hue comes from the pictures, and every picture was rendered with open weights and ships with its receipt. Nothing hangs or plays without a real take behind it, and the build has no CSS glow or glass, no system display faces, no glyph icons and no kickers.

Density depends on the room. The Lobby is a room you are persuaded in: generous gutters (`lobby-gutter`), 32 to 52px between sections, posters five to a row on a wall with 32 to 44px between rows, gallery frames four to a row. The stations are working-monitor density: 32px controls, 12px gutters on the stage, 16px in the dock, an 8-point rhythm with a 4-point half step. The Lobby moves more than the stations, and every motion in it is physical. On the first visit of a session the screen's black masking parts from the middle, and as the room scrolls up the screen sinks a little behind it. The marquee's letters hang one by one and later drop and rehang to change a count, the dust in the projector beam drifts, the posters and the gallery frames rise in a wave the first time they come into view, a live poster's leak passes once and its ticket rises, a few posters at a time wake on their own, the mouse scrubs a take across its frame and the latest takes drift past. In the stations nothing moves on load except a 180 ms fade, a take crossfades into the next, the bulbs light as a render progresses and the stage's camera moves only because the footage does. Reduced motion and Save-Data hold every state still.

**Key Characteristics:**
- Lacquer-black warm charcoal ground; the interface's colours are black, marquee white on the board (card stock its printed twin), ticket orange for the one action, brass for trim and passes; every other hue is a picture
- The Lobby: the trailer across the room over a reel of bulbs, a marquee of hung letters carrying true counts, one-sheets in brass-trimmed cases that come alive into their recorded takes, galleries of camera moves and effects whose frames the mouse scrubs, lobby cards on card stock
- The screening room: the LED wall and the stage view with the travelling camera are the interface; the dock reads as a camera report
- Barlow Condensed for what a cinema prints or hangs (marquee letters, poster, card and gallery titles, billing, the stamp, stub heads); Barlow for words; Overpass Mono tabular for every figure
- Lobby atoms in the stations: roll-ticket primary buttons, the receipt as a ticket stub, a bulb row for progress, rendered props on the rail, rendered sets in empty rooms
- Every raster rendered with open weights on our own GPU and listed with its provenance in `art/manifest.json`; text, icons and labels stay HTML and CSS
- Passes are brass and say so in words; drift is amber; failure is red
- Equipment edges in the stations (2 to 4px corners, tape chips, hairlines); print in the lobby is square and cut, never rounded
- AA contrast, keyboard operable (a "?" sheet lists every key), reduced motion respected

### Named Rules
**The Four Colours Rule.** The interface owns four colours: lacquer black (the charcoal ramp from `screen` to `pressed`), marquee white on the rendered letter board (with card stock as its printed twin), ticket orange for the one action, and brass for trim and passes. Amber and red are state. Anything else on the screen is a picture.

**The Colour Comes From The Pictures Rule.** No surface, panel, chip, icon or line of type carries a hue beyond the four colours and the two state colours. Key art, loops, footage, stills, cast photos and the rail's props carry the colour, with at most a light grade (key art at brightness 0.92 and saturation 0.95 at rest, brightened on a live poster; rail props at saturation 0.7 until hovered) and never a tint. Where type or a line must read over a picture it gets a printed shadow inside the picture, a dark halo, a scrim or an opaque dark card.

**The One Action Colour Rule.** Ticket orange appears only on the object being acted on or watched: the primary action, the ticket rising on the live poster, the lit bulbs, the reel's playhead, the scrub line under the hand, the chosen path's travelled part with its camera and marks, the active room, focus and selection, the running tally. A toggle that is on is pressed in grey (`pressed`, `control-line` edge). A chosen gallery filter is pressed grey with the orange line of a chosen tab along its foot. An informational chip is neutral with a dot. If two things on a screen are orange and neither is under the eye, one is wrong.

**The Passes Are Brass And Speak Rule.** A pass is brass and always carries its word (Open weights only, Checksum matches, Same person, Correct). Drift is amber and failure is red, and neither is ever shown by colour alone. Every tally, chip and step mark pairs its colour with a word or a dot shape, the identity reading prints its number and marks the pass line with a tick, and a reached tape mark changes fill and ink as well as hue.

**The Primary Goes Grey Rule.** A primary action that is not the action of the moment becomes a raised grey case with a strong hairline: `text-3` marks when it is disabled, `text-2` marks (`.is-quiet`, still a working button) while the take it owns is rendering or waiting for approval. It keeps its notched ticket shape; it is never a dimmed, muddy orange.

**The Flags Gate Rule.** The catalog's flags decide what the Lobby shows. A poster hangs only when its feature's real render held, or when it is a room of its own with no flag. A camera move or an effect plays in a gallery only when its flag is set and its take has a loop, and the marquee counts only those. The interface prints no tested labels: nothing untested hangs, plays or is counted, and a new option stays off the wall until a real render of it is checked.

### Named Rules
**The Condensed Is Print Rule.** Barlow Condensed sets only what a cinema would print or hang: marquee letters, the Lobby's headline and signs, poster, card and gallery titles, billing, the stamp and stub heads, the credits. It never sets a control, a field, a dock head or a sentence of interface copy; those stay Barlow, and the roll-ticket primary's label is Barlow too.

**The One Billing Order Rule.** Every ticket and stub prints its facts in one order and never guesses: seed, steps, length, recipe, audit (`lib/billing.js`). The receipt and a move page add GPU time and its cost at $0.71 an hour after them, once the receipt has recorded the time. A field the take did not record is left out, and steps appear only for takes whose stills were rendered. A poster's billing prints its models, then "showing since" the date of its proof take.

**The Every Figure In Mono Rule.** Timecode, coordinates, seeds, hashes, counts, sizes, durations and identity scores are set in Overpass Mono with tabular numerals, including the tape-mark numbers inside the stage view, the plan line, a section head's meta, a card's number, the ticket's, the stub's and a move page's values, the trailer chip, a gallery filter's count and the Lobby's proof line. Marquee letters are the one exception: a count hung on the board is matter, set in the board's condensed letters. A number in Barlow is a defect.

**The Camera-Report Label Rule.** A field label is 0.75rem, 500, uppercase, tracked 0.08em, in `text-3`; the value beside it is mono in `text`. Labels never get larger, heavier or coloured to make a hierarchy; position and the value do that. The heads above the labels are not labels: they are Barlow 600 at 0.9375rem in `text`, sentence case, with their values as mono meta, so the dock has exactly two levels, a sentence-case head and its tracked labels.

**The Sentence Is Not A Readout Rule.** A note inside a readout (the plan line's warning, the locked note, the re-roll control under a still) drops back to Barlow 0.8125rem, untracked, sentence case; a long one is shortened and carries its full sentence as a title.

**The Abbreviation Speaks Its Full Name Rule.** A rocker may print a stop as an abbreviation when its stops will not fit as words (the Around dial's F FR R BR B BL L FL in 308px; Up for elevated, Mid for medium), but every abbreviated stop carries its full name as the button's `aria-label` and `title` ("Front-right quarter", "Close-up"), and the full names are printed again, untracked, in the readout beneath the dials and in the plan line, so nobody has to decode the tape.

### Named Rules
**The Hairline Not Box Rule.** In the stations, regions are separated by a 1px `line` rule, not enclosed. The shot card is a hairline under the subject field; the filmstrip sits under a hairline; the take list and candidate set have a top and bottom rule and no fill; the dock sections and the stills strip are rules. A filled panel is reserved for things that genuinely float (menus, notices, the sheets, the reel bar) or that are objects (cast cards, move cards, raised buttons, the stage-view figure, the stage inset, and the receipt stub, which is a printed ticket).

**The Lobby Gives Back The Column Rule.** The Lobby has no dock, no Take tab and no stage padding; it is the one room that scrolls as a page. Every other room keeps the stage grid and the dock.

**The Screen Leads Rule.** The Lobby opens on footage. The screen runs the width of the room at the top of the front, at every width, and every gallery frame plays a recorded take, so the first thing anyone sees moving is a take the studio made.

**The Wall Owns The Centre Rule.** The LED wall takes at least 60% of the centre width at 1440 and all the height the shot card, monitor head, transport and filmstrip leave it. Nothing else in the stage column grows; the shot card is one row, the monitor head is one line and the filmstrip is one row of frames so the wall gets the height. In cinema it gets the window.

## Elevation & Depth

Two depth systems, one per kind of room. The stations are flat and tonal. Depth there is five steps of near-black warm charcoal (`screen` < `ground` < `panel` < `raised` < `hover` < `pressed`) separated by gaffer hairlines. Interactive surfaces step up one tone on hover and one more on press, objects (filmstrip frames, move cards, cast cards, a cast card's take frames) lift 1px under the pointer, and the only drop shadow is for things that float. The Lobby hangs things on a wall. The one-sheets, the gallery frames, the quiz's screen, lobby cards, programme cards and an empty room's rendered set carry a soft case shadow because they are objects hung in the dark. A case is built from box-shadow rings (a black mat and a 1px brass trim), so it never shifts layout. Light in the Lobby is rendered: the projector beam and the dust over the front and the light leak across a live poster are our own renders blended with `screen` over the dark.

### Named Rules
**The Hung And Floating Cast Shadows Rule.** A shadow means the thing hangs on the lobby's wall or floats above the stage. Hung things are a case (poster, screen, gallery frame, the quiz's screen), a lobby or programme card and an empty room's picture, and they take the case shadow. Floating things are a menu, notice, sheet, reel bar or floating dock, and they take the float shadow. Controls, station tiles, the wall and the dock never carry one. There are no hard offset shadows and no glow, except the warm light at the foot of a rail prop under the hand (see the rail). Halos and rims under marks over footage are the dark colour.

**The Rendered Light Rule.** Light in the Lobby comes from renders (`ov-beams`, `ov-dust`, `ov-leak`, blended with `screen` at 0.42 to 0.85) or from the art itself; never a CSS glow, bloom, glass or backdrop blur. The only drawn gradients are a picture's printed shadow under its type, the fader's hard-stop fill, the pending Shots tile's shimmer, the marquee's chasing light (seen only through its bulbs), and masks that cut shape (the ticket notches, key art falling away into the sheet, the beam's fade, the bulbs, the round fall-off of a rail prop, the edge fade of the drifting latest takes).

**The Scrim Not Blur Rule.** Anything printed on footage sits on a 78% dark scrim (92% for the running status block, a move card's glyph chip, the trailer chip and the sound switch) or under a printed foot; the sheets sit on the same scrim over the app; there is no backdrop blur or glass anywhere.

## Shapes

Two form languages. Print in the Lobby is square and cut. The screen, one-sheets, gallery frames, lobby and programme cards, the rising ticket, the receipt stub and the Recorded take stamp have no radius at all. A ticket is cut, never rounded. The roll-ticket primary has a semicircular notch cut from the middle of each end (5px radius, 6px on the large one), the rising ticket 5px and the receipt stub 7px, with a dashed tear line (1.5px dashed `card-ink` at 35%, 8px in) down the stub's left edge, 1px dashed rules between its rows and a 1px dashed rule above a ticket's billing. The stamp is tilted -4 degrees, and a card's title band sits under a 2px `card-ink` rule. Progress bulbs are 2.4px-radius dots at an 11px pitch, and the screen's reel is the same row of bulbs, one cut per segment, 3px apart. A rail prop melts into the rail through a round fall-off mask (solid to 80% of its closest side), except the Lobby's marquee strip, whose shape is the point.

In the stations, equipment edges: corners are 2 to 4px, never larger. `radius-s` (3px) is the default for controls, tiles, the stage-view figure, the running status block, the step ribbon and the skip link; `radius-m` (4px) for floating panels, cards, drop zones, the sheets and the reel bar; `radius-pill` is also 3px, so a status chip is a strip of tape, not a pill. Literal 2px corners appear on everything that touches the footage or is small: the wall, thumbnails, stage cards, cast-take frames, tape chips, badges, OSD readouts, the stage inset, a move card's glyph chip, a move tile's name over its poster, the switch thumb, the fader thumb, key hints, rocker segments. The brand mark has no box at all: a 16 by 3 bar over a 3 by 9 stem with 0.5-unit corners, floor tape laid as a T; the favicon is the same T on a `ground` square. The shot card, the take list and the candidate set have no corners at all; they are rules. The only circles are dots (status, tally, the camera's lens, the floor dot, the tape marks, a move tile's start mark, the subject's head), radios, the 64px big-play button on the wall. The subject's floor mark is a flat ellipse. The Lobby's sound switch is the one pill on the screen: 30px tall, 999px corners.

Lines are 1px solid; 1px dashed on drop zones; 1px dashed orange (`4 3`) for the 9:16 guide on the wall; `3 3` for the faint outer frame of a zoom glyph. On the stage view the strokes are plan units in a 100 by 62 box: the floor grid, the subject's ellipse, the marks' edges and the camera's frame, rays and lens are 1px non-scaling; the subject post 1.5px non-scaling; the path 0.5 (about 1.5px across the dock) with the travelled part over it at 0.75; the drop line 0.3 dashed `1 0.8`; a hollow camera's frame 0.3 dashed `1.2 0.8`. Inside the inset the path, lit part and drop line go to 0.8 / 1.1 / 0.5 so they stay legible at 140px. A move tile's track is 2px with a 2.5px post; a plain glyph is 2.5px. The identity scale is a 3px bar with a 1px by 9px tick at the pass line; the scrubber's ticks are 1px by 7px.

Imagery is always cropped to a frame. The screen and a move page's take use 1280:704, the takes' own frame (the 1024 by 564 trailer is cover-fitted into it). A one-sheet is 27:40, with an app's square key art hung at its top and falling away into the dark from 62% down. The marquee board is 7:2. Gallery frames, lobby and programme card pictures, the empty-room art, footage, stills, stage cards, thumbnails, move-card and move-tile posters and the takes under a cast card are 16:9, or 9:16 when the shot is portrait. Cast photos are 3:4 and cast candidates 1:1. The wall uses `object-fit: contain` (and so does the outgoing frame during a crossfade), and everything else uses `cover`.

## Components

The Lobby speaks the grammar of a cinema's front of house (a screen and its masking, a one-sheet in its case, a marquee, a lobby card, a ticket); the stations speak equipment grammar (a strip of tape, a rocker, a fader, a case) with the ticket as their one borrowed atom. All transitions are 120 ms on the `--ease` curve unless stated.

### Pictures and provenance
- Every raster and loop in `openfield/web/art` was rendered on the project's own rented GPU with open weights through the app's own audited templates (Qwen-Image 2.1 `qwen21_t2i` for stills, LTX 2.5 `ltx25_flf2v` for loops), then resized for the web. `art/manifest.json` lists every shipped file with its size, bytes and sha256, and the render it was made from: kind, prompt, seed, render size, template, workflow sha256, models, the open-weights audit result and the source's sha256. Each still also ships a prompt sidecar beside it (`<file>.json`: the prompt and when it was made).
- Pictures carry no words: every prompt ends "No text, no letters, no numbers, no logos, no watermark". Titles, counts, labels, icons and the brand mark are HTML, CSS and inline SVG over the pictures.
- Families: `poster-<app>` (key art), `marquee`, `art-<step>` (programme cards and empty rooms), `btn-<room>` (rail props), `ov-*` (rendered light, screen-blended) and `trailer` (the screen's cut of recorded takes, its segments in `art/trailer.json`, listed in the manifest with the rest).
- **The pixel rule.** Every recorded take ships three loops: 384px, 768px and its own width up to 1280px, never wider than the take (`openfield/previews.py`), each with a first-frame poster; each still also has a 768px copy (`stillThumb`). A frame drawn with a width, or with `hd` (640 CSS px), gets the narrowest copy that fills its pixels at the screen's density, up to 2x, else the widest (`lib/pictures.js`: `pick` for loops, `posterAttrs` with `srcset` for the posters of list rows, filmstrip frames and move cards). A hover clip asks for its frame's own width, and a tile that already plays its loop keeps that loop. A phone (under 720px) or a browser saving data gets the 384px loop whatever the density, and a phone is offered the 384 and 768px posters only. A card never shows the full `poster.png` or a full still: the Shots board paints from the 768px copies.

### Motion
- A room change is a view transition: the stage crossfades (the old room out over 180 ms, the new one in over 320 ms), the rail mark slides and the arriving prop clicks in. The stage carries its transition name only for the capture of the old room (`:root.room-swap`, taken off as soon as the new room is painted, because a named element is not hit-testable): the old picture fades out over the live new room, which answers clicks at once, and the floating layers over it (the notices, the backend menu, a sheet's scrim, and the dock where it floats at 1179px and under) are named for that capture too, so they stay on top. Without View Transitions the station's own 180 ms fade stands in; held still, the room cuts. Re-ranks glide 180 ms in Takes and Cast; approval stills enter 120 ms apart by 40 ms; dock sections fade in 120 ms and leave at once; the segmented control's inset edge fades with its fill; Shots tiles keep their identity across clicks and lift 1px on hover; chat messages settle in over 180 ms from 4px below (WAAPI, never for history), the live message ends in a 6px accent dot breathing with `pulse`, and the log glides to its end only when something new was added. Held still (reduced motion, or Save-Data through `:root.hold-still`) every animation and transition collapses to its end with no delay, nothing is armed for a reveal, cuts replace fades and no loop plays. In the other rooms the dust stands still, and the house light and the prop's click are off.
- One curve, `--ease: cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)`. 120 ms (`--t-fast`) for colour and edge changes, poster opacity, the 1px lifts, the player and OSD fade-in, the scrub line, the opacity of entering menus, notices and sheets, the whole exit of menus and sheets (a menu leaves 4px up, a sheet drops 8px), the room crossfade's fade-out, a path chip's appearance and a hover loop's fade-in. 180 ms (`--t-base`) for the dock slide (the grid's columns), the outgoing take's crossfade, the switch, the chevron, the guide fade, the standby stage's fade-in, the station fade-in, cases and cards in the Lobby, and the settle of menus (from 4px above), notices (from 8px above) and sheets (from 8px below), the room crossfade's fade-in, the house light's coming and going, the arriving prop's click, and a move page's frame in flight (a view transition). 400 ms for progress. In the Lobby the screen's masking parts once a session (900 ms after 280 ms), the marquee's letters hang (520 ms each, 26 ms apart) and drop to change a count (420 ms, every 5.6 s), the beam's dust drifts (38 s, alternate), the wall and the camera moves rise in a wave (560 ms), a filter change reflows the grid (460 ms FLIP, entering frames 380 ms), a live poster's leak passes once (900 ms), its take fades in (420 ms), its stamp appears (200 ms after 160 ms) and its ticket rises (260 ms after 120 ms), the rotating display wakes a poster every 3 s, and the latest takes drift at a steady pace (at least 40 s a loop). Station entrances use `@starting-style` with transitions only, so they are CSS, cost nothing on load and collapse under reduced motion. The stage view's camera, lit path and marks move only with the footage's own time (`seek(t)`), never on a timer, on the wall and on a move page alike. Continuous animations in the stations are the running tally pulse (1.2 s), the active step pulse (1.6 s), the indeterminate slide (1.5 s) and the skeleton breath (1.6 s). `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` collapses every animation and transition to 0.001 ms and a single iteration. Save-Data holds the Lobby's CSS loops still the same way (the dust, the hanging letters, the leak, the rise, the masking, the drift) through `:root.hold-still`, which `main.js` sets from `holdStill()`.
- **The rooms' light.** The six rooms share one light (`.ambient`, `ui/ambient.js`, `layout.css`): a still projector beam (`ov-beams`, at 0.28, fading out downward) and its dust (`ov-dust`, at 0.4, drifting 3% to the left and 2% down while it grows from 1.04 to 1.12, over 36 s, alternate). Both are screen-blended in the stage's cell at z-index -1 and clipped to the stage at every width, so they never paint over the rail, the slate bar or the dock. A house light (`ov-leak` in an 1100px round fall-off, at 0.8, fading in and out over 180 ms) follows the mouse across the stage, 18% of the way each frame, as one composited transform written only while the mouse moves. It runs only for a mouse on a window 900px or wider that hovers, and it is off while a sheet is open (`.app[inert]`), in cinema and when the pointer leaves the stage. The stage drops its `--ground` floor at 720px and up outside the Lobby and cinema, so the light shows. The Lobby keeps its own beam, dust and grain and has no house light. Under 720px the rooms have no light. Held still, the dust stands and the house light is off.
- **Sound.** Sound is opt-in and is not motion: reduced motion and Save-Data leave the studio's clicks alone, and the trailer never starts with its sound on by itself after a reload. The cues (`ui/sound.js`) are generated, not files: a clapper (a 60 ms breath of noise, band-passed from 1800 to 600 Hz) on a room change; a tape tick (30 ms at 2.4 kHz) on a rocker, switch, pressed toggle, tab or gallery filter, and on each arrow key inside a radio group; a thunk (80 ms, 160 to 70 Hz) on a primary button; a bulb's ping (80 ms at 2637 Hz) on a right answer in Name the move; and a low tick (50 ms, a square wave from 220 to 180 Hz) on a wrong one. Every cue lasts at most 80 ms and peaks at -20 dBFS. None sounds within 60 ms of another, in a hidden tab or on a held arrow key, and a click's own cue comes before the room cue it starts, so Start directing is one thunk.
- **Hover play.** Under the pointer or keyboard focus that shows (`:focus-visible`) every tile that shows a take plays its loop, muted and looping: a Lobby poster (it comes alive into its take), a lobby card or a show card (pinned while the hand is on it, `playUnderHand` in `ui/lobby.js`), a Takes wall tile or row, a filmstrip frame, a cast card's take, a Shots source, a Compare or Transition candidate, a move tile and a move card. A tile's loop takes a pinned slot of the shared decoders (`ui/wall.js`) and lets it go on leaving, and a hover clip fades in over its still in 120 ms. A still that cannot play (a Shots setup, a cast portrait) leans in 1.5% under the hand instead, over 600 ms; the stage cards and the programme cards keep still. The mouse scrubs gallery frames and Takes wall tiles. On touch a tap starts no tile's loop, except a Lobby poster, which comes alive when a pointer enters it (a tap counts). Reduced motion and Save-Data stop all of it.

### Accessibility
- AA contrast on every text and control edge (`text-3` on `ground`, `control-line` at 3:1); focus is always a 2px `accent` outline; a skip link to the stage; `sr-only` station headings; notices in a polite live region; sheets are modal dialogs that take focus on open and return it the moment they are closed (the exit fade never holds focus or the page behind); every state carries a word or shape as well as a colour; the identity reading is an image with its label and score as its name; the stage view is an image labelled "Planned camera for [move]"; filmstrip tiles and a cast card's takes carry the take name as their accessible name; the scrubber, rockers, switches and tiles are native buttons or inputs with ARIA state; an abbreviated rocker stop carries its full name as `aria-label` and `title`; the camera-position readout is a polite live region, and the stage drag is a convenience over the dials, which do the same with arrow keys; every pointer action has a key, listed under "?"; reduced motion and Save-Data honoured. In the Lobby: a poster is a link that comes alive on focus exactly as on hover. The marquee's letters are `aria-hidden` and one `sr-only` sentence speaks for the board. The ticket and stamp are `aria-hidden` and the poster's title names the take. Each reel cut is a button named "Play from" its take, the sound switch says what it does and carries `aria-pressed`, and the chip names the take it opens. Sound carries nothing alone: every cue repeats a change that already shows (the room, the pressed state, the words Correct and Not this one). The Sound switch names itself ('Sound: the takes' own sound and the studio's clicks'), carries `aria-pressed` and shows its state as a word and an icon, and nothing sounds before it is on. Gallery filters are toggle buttons with `aria-pressed` in a labelled group, and the move page holds focus to hear the arrow keys. The scrub is a convenience for the mouse, and touch and the keyboard keep the plain loop. The drifting cards' second run is hidden from readers and the keyboard. Pictures are decorative (`alt=""`) because the words beside them carry the meaning, and sections are labelled by their signs. Reduced motion and Save-Data stop the masking, the hanging letters, the dust, the leak, the rise, the drift, the rotating display, the scrub and the poster video. They also stop the rooms' dust and house light, the room crossfade (a cut), the leaving of sheets and menus, the prop's click, the frame's flight, the hover loops and the path chips. The rooms' light and the path chips are `aria-hidden` and catch no pointer, and the house light answers the mouse only. Hover play and the path chip follow keyboard focus (`:focus-visible`) as they follow the pointer, and the clip and the chip are out of the tab order.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:
- **Do** hang every app as a one-sheet (square key art, title in condensed capitals, tagline, billing) whose proof is a recorded take, and let it come alive into that take with its ticket on hover and on focus.
- **Do** open the Lobby on the screen: the trailer of recorded takes in a cased 1280:704 frame, a chip naming the take on screen, the reel of bulbs lit to the playhead, the masking parting once a session.
- **Do** let every gallery frame play its proof take and let the mouse scrub it, with a 3px `accent` line along the foot, and give every camera move a page of its own with the stage view drawn in step with the take.
- **Do** keep the palette to the four colours: lacquer black, marquee white on the board (card stock as its print), ticket orange for the one action, brass for trim and passes; let the pictures carry every other hue.
- **Do** print passes in brass with their word (Open weights only, Checksum matches, Same person); drift in amber, failure in red.
- **Do** ship every raster and loop with its provenance in `art/manifest.json` (file, size, sha256, and the render's kind, prompt, seed, template, workflow sha256, models, audit and source sha256) and a prompt sidecar beside each still; keep text, icons and labels in HTML and CSS over the pictures.
- **Do** print billing in one order everywhere (seed, steps, length, recipe, audit) and leave out what the take did not record.
- **Do** carry only counts read from the data on the marquee and the proof line.
- **Do** cut tickets, never round them: notched ends on the primary (5px, 6px large), the rising ticket (5px) and the receipt stub (7px), square corners on all lobby print.
- **Do** keep ticket orange for the object under the eye only: the primary action, the ticket on a live poster, the lit bulbs, the travelled path with its camera and reached marks, the active room, focus, selection, the running tally.
- **Do** set every figure (timecode, coordinates, seeds, hashes, counts, durations, scores, the tape-mark numbers, a section head's meta) in Overpass Mono with tabular numerals.
- **Do** draw the stage as one orthographic view (azimuth 58°, elevation 24°, floor -0.5) in a 100 by 62 box, non-scaling 1px strokes for grid, marks and camera, the path in `text-3` with the travelled part lit in `accent`, and let only the footage's time move it.
- **Do** play a move tile's loop under its glyph at rest only when the move's flag is set (0.5, 0.92 on hover, focus or when checked), keep the drawing alone otherwise with the poster frame as a response (0.75 on hover or focus, 0.85 when checked), and print the line in `on-media` with a dark halo and the name on a scrim chip whenever footage shows.
- **Do** separate regions with 1px `line` hairlines; enclose only things that float or are objects.
- **Do** give the dock two levels only: a sentence-case Barlow 600 head with mono meta, then tracked 0.75rem labels in `text-3` over mono values in `text`.
- **Do** lay the Take panel out as a receipt: the ticket stub first (RECEIPT, the take id, the billing, then the three verdict rows), the stills strip, one lead action (a large secondary when it is not the action of the moment), then the detail sections.
- **Do** re-rank take rows in place and keep a changed state lit (`accent-soft`, the tally, a tone chip) until it is noticed.
- **Do** keep corners at 2 to 4px, status chips as tape strips (3px), and the only circles as dots, radios and the big play; the brand mark has no box.
- **Do** step station surfaces by tone (`raised` to `hover` to `pressed`) for interaction, show a toggle that is on as `pressed` with a `control-line` edge, lift objects 1px under the pointer, reserve `--shadow-float` for menus, notices, sheets, the reel bar and the floating dock, and `--case-shadow` for things hung in the Lobby and an empty room's picture.
- **Do** print anything on footage on a `scrim` chip in `on-media` mono; a name on a photo sits on a scrim strip; the stage over footage sits on an opaque `screen` card with an `on-media-line` edge.
- **Do** label any synthetic or replayed frame on the wall with its tally state (amber) in words; ship no synthetic frames.
- **Do** keep every station entrance a CSS `@starting-style` transition where no view transition runs (the station fades over 180 ms; menus, notices and sheets settle over 180 ms and fade over 120 ms), crossfade rooms (180 ms out, 320 ms in) and takes (180 ms), show progress as a bulb row, honour reduced motion and Save-Data, and keep a 2px orange focus outline on every control (darkened orange on card stock).
- **Do** record a camera position as three camera-report fields (Around, Height, Distance) whose dials are the ordinary rocker, print the full position in words beneath them, and let the stage figure drag as a second way to set the same dials (grab cursor, 36px per stop, `pan-y` on touch).
- **Do** name an approval card by its role only with the evidence beside it ("Angled still" with the position, "End state" with the transformation), and fall back to "Hero still" / "Anchor N" otherwise.
- **Do** let the catalog's flags decide what hangs, plays and is counted in the Lobby, and print no tested labels.
- **Do** light a room from behind: rendered dust and beams screen-blended under the stage, a house light that follows the hand, a leak on a live poster; let every room change crossfade where the browser has View Transitions, every sheet and menu leave as it came, and every prop wake under the hand and click into place when its room arrives.
- **Do** keep sound opt-in, synthesised, short and quiet: one remembered switch, -20 dBFS, at most 80 ms, nothing in a hidden tab, and never a meaning the page does not also show.
- **Do** give each frame that asks for a width the copy that fills its pixels on the screen it is on (`lib/pictures.js`), from the three loops `openfield/previews.py` cuts, and never make a copy wider than its take.

### Don't:
- **Don't** hang a poster or a gallery frame without a real recorded take behind it, or hang, play or count anything whose flag is not set.
- **Don't** print a tested label, a status band or a Coming soon wall in the Lobby. The flags gate what hangs and plays, and the room says nothing more about testing.
- **Don't** put words, numbers or logos inside a render, or ship a raster without its manifest entry; text, counts, icons and labels are HTML and CSS.
- **Don't** set controls, fields, dock heads or interface sentences in Barlow Condensed; it is print only.
- **Don't** use green anywhere; a pass is brass.
- **Don't** use blue-black or cool greys; every dark surface is warm charcoal from the `screen` to `pressed` ramp, and card stock is the only light one.
- **Don't** add CSS glows, blooms, glass or backdrop blur; light in the Lobby and the rooms is a screen-blended render, and the only drawn gradients are a picture's printed shadow under its type, the fader's hard-stop fill, the pending shimmer and shape masks.
- **Don't** put a second accent or a hue on a resting surface, chip, icon or panel; brass is trim and passes, amber and red are state, and an informational chip (Recorded take, Keyframed, Generated) is neutral with a dot.
- **Don't** make a pressed toggle orange; loop, 9:16 and Play all read as pressed in, grey.
- **Don't** put a key hint on the primary button; the shortcut lives in its title and in the "?" sheet.
- **Don't** set numbers in Barlow or use proportional figures anywhere.
- **Don't** use pills (999px) other than the Lobby's sound switch, corners above 4px, hard offset shadows, or shadows on station cards, tiles and controls; only things hung in the Lobby (and an empty room's picture) carry the case shadow.
- **Don't** box the shot card, the stills strip, the take list, the candidate set or the dock sections; they are bounded by hairlines. Since round 16 the Take pane itself is the printed object: it is card stock, torn off at the foot, and the receipt is ruled type on that sheet rather than a stub laid on a dark surface. Nothing else in the dock is printed, and no second card is laid on the sheet. `.on-stock` is the material, not one pane: round 17 gave the Chat room's call sheet the same stock, and the die cut is two mask layers, each with its own position and size, because a mask layer carries only one position.
- **Don't** print a take's name under its filmstrip tile; the frame is the tile and the name is its tooltip.
- **Don't** dim a disabled or waiting primary to muddy orange; it goes grey (`raised`, `line-strong`, `text-3` disabled / `text-2` quiet).
- **Don't** show footage under a move tile's track at rest when the move's flag is not set, and don't scale the stage view's marks or strokes with its size. They are fixed on screen (1.35x in the dense inset only).
- **Don't** draw flat top or side plan views, a gauge or a ring for the identity score; the stage is one three-quarter view and the score is a figure over a hairline scale with the pass line as a tick.
- **Don't** set section heads as tracked capitals; the only tracked capitals are field labels, tabs, chips and the primary button.
- **Don't** use system display faces, glyph or emoji icons, kickers or eyebrows above headings, or decorative labels.
- **Don't** let the dock or filmstrip take height from the wall at 1440; in the stations the wall is the largest thing on screen.
- **Don't** ship audio files, or play a sound before the person has turned sound on.
- **Don't** name the page root, or the stage outside a room change, for a view transition. Only the rail mark and a carried frame travel at all times; during a room change the stage, the notices, the backend menu, a sheet's scrim and the floating dock travel too.
- **Don't** explain the pipeline, name a model inside a sentence, or quote a price, a GPU or a spec where a visitor reads; models are data (the starring line, billing, receipts), and a necessary state is one short line.
- **Don't** print an abbreviated stop without its full name as the button's `aria-label` and `title`, and don't abbreviate where the words fit; don't colour a set dial, a dial that is set is pressed grey like every other rocker.
- **Don't** show a sheet hint or Play all over an empty sheet; the empty state carries its own sentence.
